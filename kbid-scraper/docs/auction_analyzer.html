Executive Summary

This document describes the Auction Profit Analyzer end-to-end at a conceptual level. It explains the workflow, the logic applied at each stage, the data flows and the decision-making framework used to identify Expected Value Positive (EV+) auction lots.

The content is intended to be read top-to-bottom by product owners, engineers, and operators who need a clear, non-technical understanding of how the system arrives at buy/pass recommendations.

ℹ️

Purpose: This system automates the discovery and evaluation of auction arbitrage opportunities, combining AI-powered market research with sophisticated profit modeling to surface the highest-value resale prospects.

System Purpose

The system's core purpose is to analyze scraped auction listings and surface resale opportunities that are likely to be profitable after accounting for purchase costs, fees, shipping, and risk.

It synthesizes market signals to produce a ranked list of opportunities and a short, actionable recommendation for each item.

Key Objectives

  • Speed: Process hundreds of auction listings in minutes, not hours
  • Accuracy: Leverage real-time market data to estimate true resale value
  • Risk Management: Identify and quantify uncertainty to avoid bad bets
  • Actionability: Provide clear buy/pass recommendations with supporting rationale
  • Scalability: Handle multiple auctions and categories simultaneously

High-Level Architecture

The solution is organized as a linear analysis pipeline with clear stages and handoffs. Each stage transforms data and passes enriched records to the next component.

Pipeline Components

  • Input Ingestion: Accepts cleaned CSV exports from auction scrapers containing lot details, current bids, descriptions, and metadata.
  • Data Normalization: Transforms raw fields into canonical item records with standardized formats and extracted attributes.
  • Market Researcher: External research component powered by AI that gathers comparable pricing, demand signals, and market trends.
  • Profit Engine: Calculates expected buy price, total costs, expected sell price, net profit and ROI using condition-aware modeling.
  • Scoring & Risk: Applies a multi-factor scoring model to rank opportunities (0-100 scale) and compute risk penalties.
  • Output Writer: Produces machine-readable artifacts (JSON records) and human-readable summaries (CSV, reports) for decision-making.
✓

Design Philosophy: The pipeline is designed for transparency and auditability. Every decision includes the signals and intermediate values used, enabling analysts to reproduce or challenge any recommendation.

Data Inputs

The primary input is a CSV file of auction listings. Each row represents a lot and typically contains attributes such as:

  • Lot number: Unique identifier for tracking
  • Title & description: Free-form text describing the item
  • Current bid: Latest bid amount or starting price
  • Category: Item classification (Appliances, Electronics, Furniture, etc.)
  • URLs: Item page link and image URLs
  • Location: Seller location or pickup information
  • Condition notes: Any disclosed damage, missing parts, or wear

The system treats this CSV as the canonical source of truth for a single analysis run. Multiple CSVs can be processed sequentially for batch analysis.

⚠️

Data Quality Impact: Quality of input directly affects output reliability. Rich descriptions, explicit brand and model information, and clear condition notes materially improve downstream research accuracy and reduce risk scores.

Parsing & Normalization

Parsing converts each CSV row into a normalized internal representation suitable for downstream processing.

Normalization Steps

  • Numeric standardization: Prices and bids are converted to a canonical currency (USD cents) and numeric type for consistent calculations.
  • Text normalization: Free-form text fields (title/description) are trimmed, redundant whitespace removed, and common abbreviations expanded.
  • Field inference: Missing fields are inferred where reasonable—for example, mapping common category labels to a controlled vocabulary or extracting condition keywords from descriptions.
  • Validation: Records with clearly insufficient data are flagged for special handling (lower confidence scores, manual review, or exclusion from automated scoring).

The output is a clean, structured record ready for entity extraction and research.

Entity Extraction

Entity extraction identifies structured attributes inside textual fields: brand, model, year, capacity, condition keywords (new, used, damaged), and other product-specific identifiers.

Why Entity Extraction Matters

These attributes enable:

  • Targeted comparable searches: "Samsung 65-inch QLED TV" finds better matches than generic "TV"
  • Price adjustments: Specific models may command premiums or discounts based on desirability
  • Condition modeling: Keywords like "damaged screen" or "new in box" directly impact resale estimates
  • Confidence scoring: Well-extracted entities increase research confidence

Confidence levels are associated with each extracted attribute. Low-confidence extractions (ambiguous text, missing context) reduce downstream weighting from comparables and increase the overall risk score.

Market Research & Signals

The market research component aggregates external signals that represent current market value and demand trends. This is powered by AI (Google Gemini) which performs real-time research across multiple sources.

Research Signals Collected

  • Recent sold prices: eBay sold listings and marketplace history (considered most reliable—reflects actual transaction prices)
  • Retail prices: New-item pricing from retailers and Amazon (establishes upper bound)
  • Used-item averages: Aggregate pricing across resale channels (establishes mid-range)
  • Demand indicators: How often analogous items sell (frequency)
  • Liquidity scores: How quickly comparable items convert to sales (velocity)
  • Trend direction: Rising, stable, or falling prices based on recent comparables

Research Metadata

Each research result includes important metadata:

  • Timestamp: When the data was gathered
  • Confidence score: How reliable the estimate is (0-10)
  • Source: Which method or API provided the data
  • Sample size: Number of comparables found

The profit engine uses these signals in weighted combinations—preferring sold-listing evidence when available and falling back to broader used or retail averages when historical sold data is sparse.

Price Estimation & Bid Modeling

Price estimation is split into two forward-looking tasks that bracket the transaction:

1. Final Auction Price Estimate

A model estimates the likely final buy price given:

  • Current bid amount
  • Time remaining until auction close
  • Category-specific bidding behavior (competitive vs. sleeper items)
  • Historical bid escalation patterns

This estimate is conservative by default and may include a configurable multiplier to represent expected bid escalation in competitive lots.

2. Expected Resale Price

A condition-aware resale price is derived from comparables:

  • Sold listings inform the baseline price
  • Adjustments applied for condition (new, used, damaged)
  • Deductions for missing accessories or cosmetic issues
  • Local market factors (if applicable)

Both estimates are annotated with uncertainty ranges. Higher uncertainty leads to higher risk penalties in the scoring stage, protecting against false positives.

Cost Estimation

Comprehensive cost estimation covers all expected outflows required to resell the item. Nothing is left out—the goal is a realistic net profit figure.

Cost Categories

  • Purchase cost: The estimated final auction price (from bid modeling)
  • Platform fees: eBay final value fees (12.95%), payment processing (4.25%), or other marketplace fees
  • Payment processing: PayPal, Stripe, or platform-integrated processing fees
  • Shipping estimates: Percentage of value or category-based flat estimates (5% default for most items)
  • Refurbishment allowances: Repair costs for items with disclosed damage
  • Logistics costs: Local pickup, storage, or handling fees

Cost Confidence

The system tracks which costs are high-confidence (well-known marketplace fees) versus estimates (shipping, repair). Low-confidence cost elements increase the overall risk assessment and may trigger manual review flags.

Costs are combined into a single expected outlay number used by the profit engine in the final profit calculation.

Profit & ROI Calculation

The profit engine computes net profit and return on investment (ROI) using the estimated resale price and aggregated costs.

Key Metrics

  • Net Profit: Expected resale proceeds minus purchase price and all costs
    Net Profit = Expected Sell Price - (Final Auction Price + Fees + Shipping + Repairs)
  • ROI (%): Net profit expressed as a percentage of total capital at risk
    ROI = (Net Profit / Total Capital) × 100
  • Profit Margin: Net profit as a percentage of sell price
    Margin = (Net Profit / Expected Sell Price) × 100

Uncertainty Handling

The engine supports propagating uncertainty bands from the price and cost estimates to calculate:

  • Conservative scenario: Lower sell price, higher costs
  • Optimistic scenario: Higher sell price, lower costs
  • Expected value: Probability-weighted average

These values are central inputs to scoring and ranking—they directly determine which items rise to the top of the opportunity list.

Scoring and Risk Model

Each item receives a composite opportunity score (0–100) that reflects its attractiveness as a resale opportunity. The score combines multiple weighted factors.

Scoring Factors

  • Absolute Net Profit (40 points max): Rewards higher dollar outcomes—a $200 profit scores higher than a $50 profit
  • ROI Percentage (30 points max): Rewards high-percentage returns—helpful for capital-limited strategies where 100% ROI matters more than raw dollars
  • Market Demand (20 points max): How strong is buyer demand for comparable items (scale 0-10 from research)
  • Liquidity (10 points max): How quickly comparable items convert to sales—fast-moving inventory reduces holding costs and risk

Risk Score (1–10)

A separate risk score quantifies uncertainty and operational risk. Higher numbers mean higher risk.

Risk factors include:

  • Low research confidence (sparse comparables, uncertain pricing)
  • Poor liquidity (niche markets, slow-moving categories)
  • Ambiguous condition descriptions (hidden damage risk)
  • Large uncertainty ranges in repair cost estimates
  • Thin profit margins (small buffer for error)
  • Declining market trends (sell prices falling)

The risk score reduces the opportunity score as a penalty, or can be used independently as a gating filter for human review (e.g., "flag all items with risk > 7").

Recommendation Logic

Recommendations are derived from the final opportunity score and the risk profile. They map to human-friendly categories that guide bidding decisions.

Recommendation Bands

  • STRONG BUY — Score ≥ 70, Risk ≤ 4: Best opportunities with high profit, low risk, and strong confidence
  • BUY — Score ≥ 50, Risk ≤ 6: Good opportunities with solid profit and acceptable risk
  • MAYBE — Score ≥ 30: Marginal opportunities for experienced flippers willing to accept higher risk
  • PASS — Score < 30 or negative EV: Not recommended—insufficient profit or too much risk

The mapping is configurable and typically applies higher thresholds for automated buy recommendations while allowing lower thresholds for human review or batch purchasing strategies.

Explanatory Summaries

Recommendations always include an explanatory summary that lists the dominant factors behind the decision, for example:

  • "Excellent opportunity: High profit ($180), strong ROI (85%), low risk (3/10), excellent demand"
  • "Good opportunity but watch condition: Medium profit ($65), good ROI (42%), medium risk due to cosmetic damage"
  • "Pass: Low research confidence due to sparse comparables, uncertain resale price"

Outputs & Artifacts

The system produces structured artifacts intended for both automated workflows and human review.

Output Types

1. Full Analysis Record (JSON)

A structured record for each lot containing:

  • Normalized input (lot #, description, bid, category)
  • Extracted entities (brand, model, condition)
  • Research signals (retail, used, sold prices, demand, liquidity, trends)
  • Estimated prices and costs (final buy, expected sell, all fees)
  • Profit and ROI figures
  • Opportunity score and risk score
  • Recommendation and reasoning text

2. Summary Table (CSV)

A compact ranked list suitable for spreadsheet review with key columns:

  • Lot ID
  • Short description
  • Current bid
  • Net profit
  • ROI %
  • Opportunity score
  • Recommendation

3. Operational Logs

Timestamped records of:

  • Research queries performed
  • Confidence scores
  • Warnings or exceptional conditions
  • Processing time per item

Outputs are designed to be machine-readable and easy to filter or sort for downstream decision processes (e.g., import to spreadsheet, feed to bidding bot, trigger alerts).

Operational Considerations

Running the system at scale requires attention to several operational topics that ensure reliability and accuracy.

Data Quality

Garbage in produces garbage out. Richer descriptive fields substantially improve results. Best practices:

  • Include detailed descriptions with brand/model when scraping
  • Capture all condition notes and damage disclosures
  • Preserve image URLs for manual verification if needed

Rate Limits & Retries

External research components (Gemini API) may have rate limits and transient errors. The system handles this gracefully:

  • Research is treated as best-effort
  • Failures annotate results with low confidence rather than failing the whole run
  • Automatic retries with exponential backoff
  • Conservative rate limiting (default: 15 requests/minute)

Auditability

Each decision includes the signals and intermediate values used to compute the score so analysts can:

  • Reproduce any recommendation
  • Challenge or contest scoring
  • Understand why an item was ranked high/low
  • Track actual vs. predicted performance

Human-in-the-Loop

High-value items should be routed for manual verification. The system surfaces the items most worth human attention:

  • Strong Buy items above a dollar threshold (e.g., $500+ expected profit)
  • Items with high risk scores (e.g., risk ≥ 7)
  • Items with low research confidence

Edge Cases & Limitations

Notable limitations and edge cases that users should be aware of when interpreting results.

⚠️

Important: This system provides analysis and recommendations based on market data. Actual results may vary. Always do your own due diligence before bidding on high-value items.

Rare or Unique Items

Little or no comparable data yields low confidence and often requires manual research. The system will flag these but cannot provide reliable estimates without market history.

Poor Condition Descriptions

Vague language ("some wear", "as-is") can hide repair costs that invalidate an otherwise attractive-looking opportunity. Human review is recommended for condition-sensitive categories.

Rapidly Changing Markets

For some categories (electronics, trending items), recent trends can render historical comparables obsolete quickly. The system attempts to surface trend signals but cannot foresee sudden market shifts.

Local vs. National Markets

Some items sell differently based on locality (furniture, large appliances). Unless local pricing signals are available, the system applies general estimates which may not reflect your specific market.

Auction Competition

Final auction prices can exceed estimates in hot auctions with multiple motivated bidders. The bid multiplier attempts to account for this but cannot predict outliers.

Tuning, Validation & Monitoring

To keep the system reliable and improving over time, adopt a validation and monitoring program that includes:

Performance Tracking

  • Predicted vs. actual: Track actual sale prices and realized profit for a representative sample of historical wins and losses
  • ROI accuracy: Compare predicted ROI to actual returns
  • Win rate: Percentage of recommended items that close profitably

Calibration

  • Scoring weights: Periodically review and adjust profit/ROI/demand/liquidity weights based on empirical outcomes
  • Cost assumptions: Update fee structures and shipping multipliers as platforms change policies
  • Bid multipliers: Tune auction price escalation assumptions by category

Research Confidence Monitoring

  • Monitor research confidence trends over time
  • Add human review thresholds where confidence drops below acceptable levels
  • Identify categories with consistently poor comparables data

Audit Trail

  • Maintain an audit trail of configuration changes and experiments
  • Tag runs with version numbers so results can be attributed to parameter updates
  • Document decision logic changes for compliance

Potential improvements that increase accuracy, automation, and coverage:

Near-Term Enhancements

Glossary

EV+ (Expected Value Positive)
An item whose expected resale proceeds exceed estimated costs and purchase price by a margin that justifies the risk. The probability-weighted outcome is positive.
Liquidity
An indicator of how quickly comparable items are selling in the market. High liquidity means items convert to cash fast (days). Low liquidity means longer holding times (weeks/months).
Confidence Score
A numeric indicator (0-10) that shows how reliable a particular researched signal or extracted attribute is. Based on data quality, sample size, and recency.
Opportunity Score
A composite 0-100 metric combining profit, ROI, demand, and liquidity minus risk penalties. Higher scores indicate better arbitrage opportunities.
Risk Score
A 1-10 metric quantifying uncertainty and operational risk. Higher scores mean greater chance of loss or lower-than-expected returns.
Comparables (Comps)
Similar items that have sold recently, used as pricing references. Sold listings from eBay are considered the most reliable comparables.
Final Value Fee (FVF)
The percentage-based fee charged by selling platforms (e.g., eBay) when an item sells. Typically 12-15% of the sale price.
Bid Multiplier
A factor applied to current auction bids to estimate final purchase price. Accounts for expected bid escalation before auction close.
